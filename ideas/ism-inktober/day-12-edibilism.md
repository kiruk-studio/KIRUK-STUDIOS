## Concept

Amateur cooks (any age, from 10 to 70) add ingredients with excitement, not experience: too much chilli, oil, or extra vegetables, and the stomach pays later. Edibilism is a fun app that judges food before you taste it (a sarcastic "stomach forecast") and lets everyone rate it honestly after eating. Core message: food that isn't toxic. Roast the cook, protect the stomach.

## How it could be built

1. **Virtual kitchen:** tap ingredients from a shelf with rough amounts ("a pinch," "a spoon," "way too much").
2. **Rules engine:** each ingredient has a safe range; going over it or combining risky things lowers the forecast.
3. **Edibility scale:** the forecast lands between Edible and Call Mom, with a sarcastic line.
4. **After the meal:** everyone rates it; the app learns which combinations go wrong.
5. **Later:** pan photo input, grandma voice mode, weekly hall of shame.

v1 = steps 1 to 3.

## Open questions

- Where do the "safe ranges" come from? Needs a trustworthy source before any real-safety claim.
- Regional kitchens first (South Indian staples: sambar, dosa, rasam) or generic?
- Real danger (allergies, undercooked meat, raw kidney beans, rice left out) must read as a plain warning, never a joke.
