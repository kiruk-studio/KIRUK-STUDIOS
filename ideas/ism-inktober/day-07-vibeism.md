## Concept

A screening app where candidates and companies see their vibe side by side. People, especially Gen Z, often leave when the vibe doesn't match, which wastes time for both sides. Vibeism shows whether values align, so hires stay longer.

Quotes:

- "People don't leave jobs, they leave the version of themselves they become there."
- "Skills get you hired. Vibe makes you stay."

## The six questions the brain asks

Why people like a company's vibe:

1. **Same values:** Do they care about what I care about?
2. **Similar people:** Are they my kind of people?
3. **Feels safe:** Can I be myself and make mistakes?
4. **Growth and freedom:** Will I learn, and get to decide things?
5. **Pride:** Am I proud to say "I'm part of this"?
6. **Honest:** Do their words match their actions?

## The iceberg: said vs. felt

- **Above the line (said):** salary, title, perks.
- **Below the line (felt, never said):** being seen, respect, belonging, trust, meaning.

## The mirror test

| Reflection | Result             |
| ---------- | ------------------ |
| Better me  | I stay and love it |
| Same me    | I stay for money   |
| Smaller me | I leave            |

Test question: _"After a year here, will I like the person I see in the mirror?"_

## Example profile: the craft-driven builder

Design first, structure that scales, freedom to imagine and execute, iteration, space to learn and experiment, best of the best over ordinary.

| Need                      | Good sign                           | Warning sign                       |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Design comes first        | Taste and quality decide            | "Just ship it, nobody will notice" |
| Structure that scales     | Clear systems built to grow         | Quick hacks piled on hacks         |
| Freedom to imagine and do | Same person dreams it and builds it | Five approvals per idea            |
| Iterate                   | Version 2 is welcome                | Mistakes get blamed                |
| Learn and experiment      | Time to try new tools               | Same task on repeat                |
| Best of the best          | High bar, raised by peers           | "Good enough" is the goal          |

Mirror: _"a craftsperson getting sharper every year."_

## How the app could work

Flow: **Candidate vibe** + **Company vibe** → **Match score** → **Reality check** (employees confirm or correct) → **Stay longer** (retention data trains the score).

**Vibe spectrums** — each person and company gets a dot on each line; the gap is the match:

- Clear structure ↔ Total freedom
- Calm pace ↔ Fast pace
- Works solo ↔ Works in team
- Stability ↔ Risk and bets
- Flat, open ↔ Top-down
- Money first ↔ Mission first

Big gaps on **values** lines matter more than gaps on **style** lines. Leaning toward: aligned on values, diverse on style.

**Measurement ideas:**

- Skip MBTI-style types. Big Five is the validated model; use it lightly.
- Trade-off questions ("Pick one: fast chaotic team shipping daily, or calm team polishing for weeks").
- Scenario questions ("Friday night, a bug hits your launch. What do you do?").
- Dealbreaker list (3 things that would make them quit).
- Company profile built from current employees, not the brand page.
- Check-ins at 30, 90, and 180 days.

**Risks:**

- Companies will fake their vibe.
- Vibe matching can turn into bias. Match on values and work style, never background or age.
- Needs proof: track 6- and 12-month retention.

## Open questions

- Which spectrums are "values" and which are "style"? (Money ↔ Mission feels like values; Solo ↔ Team feels like style.)
- Who pays: candidates, companies, or neither at first?
- Smallest proof: a personal mirror-test worksheet before any two-sided app?
