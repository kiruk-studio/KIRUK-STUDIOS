## Concept

After the AI boom, everything moves fast: prompting, iterating, shipping. But the work gets sloppy, and results aren't what we expected. Turtleism isn't about being slow. It's about being clear before being fast, with product thinking: asking the right questions and dissecting an idea from the base layer up. Design often hides ten to fifteen layers before the core.

_"Before we lay a road, we need to know the right direction."_

## The four parts of the shell

Main drawing: a turtle whose shell is split into four sections, read clockwise from top left. Top half = thinking (get clear). Bottom half = understanding (go deep).

| #   | Part             | Meaning         | Note                                                              |
| --- | ---------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Askism**       | Question first  | Who is it for? What is "done"? What must it never be?             |
| 2   | **Answerism**    | Write it down   | Answer every question specifically. Vague answer = not ready yet. |
| 3   | **Dissectism**   | Peel the layers | Open the idea layer by layer; 10 to 15 layers hide the core.      |
| 4   | **Intersectism** | Find the links  | See where layers touch. Change one, see what moves.               |

Flow: **Ask → Answer → Dissect → Intersect → Build**

## Journal page

**Title**
_Day 10–11 · Turtleism_
Slow at the start. Fast at the end.
Doodle: a turtle whose shell is split into four sections.

**The issue**
Since AI, everything moves fast. We prompt fast, iterate fast, ship fast. The answers come quickly, but they're not what we wanted. Fix after fix, the work gets sloppier.
Doodle: a speed line ending in a tangled scribble.

**Why it happened**
When the next try is free, we stop thinking before trying. A vague question gets a vague answer, so we ask again and again. We fix the output instead of fixing the question.
Doodle: a loop of arrows labeled prompt → fix → prompt → fix.

**The idea**
Turtleism isn't about being slow. It's about being clear before being fast. A road built fast in the wrong direction still leads nowhere.
Doodle: a perfect road ending at a cliff, with a turtle checking a map at the start.

**The four parts** (see table above)
Doodles: a turtle holding a question mark (Askism); a checklist with one item circled "too vague" (Answerism); an onion cut in half with labeled layers (Dissectism); overlapping circles with a dot labeled "core" (Intersectism).

**The skill: how it comes in**
Turtleism becomes a habit, and even an AI skill. Before anything gets built, the AI asks questions first, writes back what it understood, breaks the idea into layers, and checks how they connect. Only then does it build.
Doodle: a turtle crossing five stepping stones over a river.

**The solution**
Each step is a gate. No moving on until the step is clear. Slow thinking at the start replaces a hundred fast fixes at the end.
Doodle: a small gate with a "clear?" sign.

**The impact**

- On the work: the first version is close to right, not the tenth.
- On time: fewer loops, less rework.
- On the team: everyone builds toward the same picture.
- On me: less frustration, more pride in what I make.

Doodle: a dizzy hare going in circles next to a calm turtle at the finish line.

**Hare vs. turtle**
Hare: prompt, fix, prompt, fix, lost.
Turtle: ask, understand, build once, it fits.

**Closing**
_The hare wins the sprint._
_The turtle arrives where it meant to go._
