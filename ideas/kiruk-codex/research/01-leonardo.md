# How Leonardo made complex ideas clear on paper

This note is about **process**, not about how the pages look. Each claim links to a source. Where I go beyond what a source says, the line is marked **(inference)**. Modern catalogue and scholarly text is summarised in my own words. The only direct quotes are short ones from Richter's public-domain 1888 translation, plus one note by Leonardo given in a modern translation.

A caveat on the Richter quotes: I pulled them from the Project Gutenberg plain text with a fetch tool and spot-checked them. Check the wording against the Gutenberg HTML before printing any of them on studio.kiruk.in.

### The working sequence (what the technical studies show)

- **Chalk or stylus first, then ink.** Iron-gall ink is transparent in infrared, which exposes the carbon underdrawing beneath it. Clayton and Donnithorne report that the underdrawings are often schematic, even perfunctory, and that he rarely changed his mind while inking. On some sheets he rubbed out the underdrawing after fixing the image in pen, probably with stale bread. On the famous fetus sheet, infrared showed a black chalk underdrawing nobody had suspected. ([Technè 2024][techne])
- **Layered stages on one sheet.** For RCIN 912281 the catalogue reconstructs the order. He set the torso outline in red, then black chalk. He folded the sheet and pricked through both halves to get a perfectly symmetrical frame. He added the internal detail, then clarified depth with pen hatching and yellow wash. Finally he pricked the whole design so it could be pounced as a template for clean copies. A thumbprint in wet ink survives at the edge. ([RCT 912281][rct-912281])
- **Notes accrete over time.** On the Anatomical Manuscript A sheets you can see a range of inks and nibs in the notes, added as his understanding changed. The page is a record of process, not one sitting. ([Clayton & Philo, _Anatomist_][rct-anat])
- **Rough sheets and fair copies are different registers.** The proportion series (e.g. RCIN 919132) is drawn schematically in pen with no underdrawing and laid out neatly. The catalogue reads it as a fair copy of rough notes made elsewhere. ([RCT 919132][rct-919132])

### Left-handed hatching

- His parallel shading lines slope **from top left to bottom right**, the natural direction for a left-hander. A right-hander's lines slope the other way. ([Getty, Brooks][getty])
- The direction the stroke _travels_ varies while the _slope_ stays constant. Ink weight shows hair-hatching begun at the bottom and pulled upward, tapering at the end. Strokes around a chin start at the profile and run downward with a small "hook return" at the end. ([Getty][getty])
- After about 1500 he worked more tonally, so the hatching becomes less obvious. Right-handed copyists imitated the slope by turning the sheet. The Getty has been offered "Leonardos" that were plainly drawn by right-handers. ([Getty][getty])

### How iron-gall ink ages

- Iron-gall ink is colourless when mixed, oxidises to a rich black, and with age often turns brown or rusty orange, depending on the recipe and how much light it has had. Very acidic batches strike through to the back of the sheet and fracture. ([Met, Ink][met-ink])
- Conservators list the signs of ink corrosion: the ink shifting from black to brown, the paper browning and turning brittle, inked lines cracking, and loss of the inked areas. ([IFLA][ifla])
- The browning starts at the inked lines and spreads outward as a **halo**, and can migrate through to the verso. ([AIC Book & Paper Group Annual v28][aic])
- "Sepia" is mostly a colour word. True cuttlefish sepia is rarely found in drawings. ([Met, Ink][met-ink])

## 2. Page composition

**Hierarchy first.** On the Manuscript A sheets the large drawings set the layout, and the smaller details and compact text blocks are fitted in around them afterwards. ([Clayton & Philo, _Anatomist_][rct-anat])

**Several studies on one sheet, and not always one subject.**

- RCIN 919007v has two spine studies, a third laid horizontally, two vertebra details drawn at a larger scale, and notes. One subject, several aspects and scales. ([RCT 919007][rct-919007])
- RCIN 919102 puts the fetus beside a binocular-vision diagram and notes on painting and mechanics. Unrelated subjects share the sheet. ([RCT 919102][rct-919102])

**Order of studies.**

- RCIN 919001v runs as a sequence from the upper right, turning the body slowly in space and ending with a frontal dissected view at the bottom. ([RCT 919001][rct-919001]) **(inference:** starting at the upper right fits a right-to-left writer.)
- RCIN 919009r starts at the lower left and climbs. Position alone would not tell you the order, so he labels the stages **1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th**, and continues 5th and 6th on another sheet (919012v). ([RCT 919009][rct-919009])
- Series run across sheet boundaries. A note on RCIN 919013 tells him what to do in "the fourth diagram", and the series continues on 919003v. ([RCT 919013][rct-919013])

**One topic, one block of page.** In Codex Madrid I each topic usually gets its own rectangular area of the sheet. When text overflows, it goes into the margin or onto a neighbouring page. On f. 67r he writes that a further effect cannot be described there for lack of space. ([Codex Madrid I, BNE texts via Google Arts & Culture][gac-madrid])

- Codex Arundel opens (f. 1r) with Leonardo calling it a collection without order, copied from many papers, which he hoped to sort by subject later. ([British Library, Arundel MS 263][bl-arundel])
- For orientation only: his habit of giving each subject its own folded sheet was later broken up by binders who cut and stacked the leaves, which is why the codex now looks random. ([Wikipedia, Codex Arundel][wiki-arundel])
- In Codex Forster I.2 some writing runs past the inner margin, so he wrote on the sheets before they were folded into a booklet. ([V&A][vam])

**Mirror writing.** His private notes run right to left. Clayton and Philo argue it was a lifelong left-hander's habit, not secrecy, and that it reads easily with a little practice. ([_Anatomist_][rct-anat]; [BL][bl-arundel]; [V&A][vam])

**Where text sits relative to the figure.** Beside it and around it, in compact blocks. On 919009r a note literally surrounds the small detail it explains. ([RCT 919009][rct-919009]) When space runs out on a working sheet, the blocks get untidy and rough lines join one passage to its continuation elsewhere. RCIN 919070 had already been torn before he wrote on it. ([RCT 919070][rct-919070])

**How notes refer to parts.**

- **Letters placed on the drawing and repeated in the text.** RCIN 919057r locates points where lettered lines cross (am × cb, rm × hf). On the verso, letters b, n and m mark the optic foramen, lacrimal fossa and sinus openings. ([RCT 919057][rct-919057]) RCIN 919001r marks subscapularis with o n m. ([RCT 919001][rct-919001]) RCIN 919009v lists the 27 bones of the hand by letters and numbers. ([RCT 919009][rct-919009]) In Madrid I the pieces are lettered from the alphabet and explained in the text alongside. ([BNE via GAC][gac-madrid]) His painting notes work the same way: "Let a b be the window, m the point of light." (Richter ¶549, [Gutenberg][gut])
- **Ordinals** for stages (1st…6th, 919009).
- **Proximity**: notes sit next to what they explain.
- **Leader lines**: none of the catalogue entries I checked describe label-to-part leader lines. The connecting lines that do appear join text blocks (919070). **(inference:** Leonardo keys by letter and adjacency. Leader lines belong to the modern annotation layer, which is a deliberate kiruk addition.)

**White space.** On working sheets space is a resource that fills up over time. On fair copies (919132) the layout is neat and airy. **(inference:** his white space is not designed. A kiruk plate should reserve it on purpose, because the working-sheet density is what makes pastiche look busy.)

Kemp's umbrella claim, summarised: in the notebooks Leonardo perfected or invented almost every illustrative technique used before X-rays. He lists systematic light and shade, sections, transparency, exploded views of body and machine, diagrams of function, and drawn thought experiments, applied across all his fields. ([Kemp, _Nature_ 2019][kemp-nature]) RCT adds that these conventions are now so routine in medical imaging that their originality is easy to miss. ([RCT story][rct-story])

## 4. His own stated method (Richter translation, public domain)

All quotes are from J. P. Richter, _The Notebooks of Leonardo da Vinci_ (1888), Project Gutenberg #5000 ([ebook][gut], [plain text][gut-txt]). ¶ = Richter's paragraph number.

**Drawing beats watching and words**

- ¶796: "And you, who say that it would be better to watch an anatomist at work than to see these drawings, you would be right" (he goes on: only if one body could show everything, and it cannot).
- ¶796: "if it were possible to observe all the things which are demonstrated in such drawings in a single figure"
- ¶798: "This depicting of mine of the human body will be as clear to you as if you had the natural man before you"
- ¶654 (to the poet): "Painting comprehends in itself all the forms of nature, while you have nothing but words"
- ¶653: "The eye, which is called the window of the soul" (a useful bridge to kiruk's eye motif)
- Not in Richter. A note on Windsor RCIN 919071r, in a modern translation: "With what words, O writer, will you describe with like perfection the entire configuration which the drawing here does?" ([Wells, _JTCVS_ 2004][wells]; sheet: [RCT 919071][rct-919071])

**Several aspects**

- ¶798: "require to see it from different aspects"
- ¶798: "considering it from below and from above and from its sides, turning it about"
- ¶798: "there will be put before you three or four demonstrations of each limb, from various points of view"

**Order of demonstration**

- ¶797: "This work must begin with the conception of man"
- ¶799: "First draw the bones, let us say, of the arm, and put in the motor muscle from the shoulder to the elbow"
- ¶800: "Begin the anatomy at the head and finish at the sole of the foot."
- ¶801 counts out the demonstrations: complete figures, then bones with nerves, then bones alone.

**Order of learning and working**

- ¶483: "The youth should first learn perspective, then the proportions of objects."
- ¶491: "do not go on to the second [step] till you have the first well fixed in memory and in practice."
- ¶492: "And note in their outlines, which way they tend;"
- ¶596: "always sketch the whole figure and then finish those limbs which seem to you the best"
- ¶579: "Let you sketches of historical pictures be swift and the working out of the limbs not be carried too far" (sic)
- ¶571: "take a note of them with slight strokes thus, in a little book which you should always carry with you."
- ¶555: "First give a general shadow to the whole of that extended part which is away from the light."
- ¶529: "When you want to see if your picture corresponds throughout with the objects you have drawn from nature, take a mirror"

**Reason over habit**

- ¶19: "Those who are in love with practice without knowledge are like the sailor who gets into a ship without rudder or compass"
- ¶20: "The painter who draws merely by practice and by eye, without any reason, is like a mirror"

**Lettering in the text itself**

- ¶549: "Let a b be the window, m the point of light."

What this adds up to **(inference):** draw first and let the drawing carry configuration. Show every structure from several aspects. Explain in a fixed order, from simple to compound and from bones outward. Sketch the whole swiftly, then finish only what matters. Key the text to the drawing with letters. Check the result against a fresh eye (the mirror).

## 5. Why the pages still read after 500 years, and what pastiche gets wrong

### Why they still read

1. **We already know the grammar.** Section, exploded view, orthogonal views and thread diagram became the standard language of technical and medical imaging, so a modern eye reads them without being taught. ([RCT story][rct-story]; [Kemp][kemp-nature])
2. **Meaning lives in line, not colour.** Monochrome contour and hatching on a light ground survive the ink turning brown, because figure–ground contrast and stroke direction still carry the form. Colour was kept out of the anatomical work on purpose. ([RCT 919102][rct-919102]; [IFLA][ifla]) **(inference** for the survival argument)
3. **Redundancy kills ambiguity.** Several views, staged layers and numbered sequences mean no single drawing has to be read perfectly. ([Richter ¶798][gut]; [RCT 919009][rct-919009])
4. **Truth under the style.** The drawings come from dozens of dissections, measurement down to millimetres, and experiments. RCT calls the 919007 spine the first accurate depiction in history, with shading subtle enough that the curvatures read clearly. ([RCT story][rct-story]; [RCT 912319][rct-912319]; [RCT 919007][rct-919007]; [Kemp][kemp-nature])
5. **Division of labour.** The drawing shows configuration. Letters bind it to notes that give cause, measurement and instruction. ([RCT 919057][rct-919057]; [Wells/919071][wells])
6. **Hierarchy.** One large figure sets the page and details are subordinate. ([_Anatomist_][rct-anat])

**Honest limit:** the _drawings_ read clearly. The _notes_ often did not. Contemporaries barely understood the dense, disorganised material, and Leonardo himself called Arundel a collection without order. ([RCT story][rct-story]; [BL][bl-arundel]) A kiruk plate should improve on that part, not copy it.

## Rules (for the kiruk-codex skill)

Each rule is a hard constraint, followed by its reason.

1. **One question per figure.** Before drawing, write the single question the plate answers. _Why:_ each Madrid I topic gets its own block, and each 919009 drawing answers one stage. Pastiche draws "a Leonardo page" instead of an answer.
2. **Draw a real, specific object with real numbers.** No invented mechanism, no ornamental parts. _Why:_ his clarity rests on dissection and measurement (912319, ¶796), and the style makes false content look true (912603's ventricles).
3. **Show at least two aspects** (orthogonal pair or a rotation series). _Why:_ ¶798 asks for three or four views per limb; 919009v gives four orthogonal views; 919005 gives eight.
4. **Pick the device from the question.** Hidden interior: section or cutaway. How parts join: exploded view. How it is built: ordered layer build-up. How it moves: phase series. What differs: side-by-side at the same scale. What forces: thread diagram. _Why:_ he borrowed each convention for a purpose (RCT anatomy essay; Kemp).
5. **Shape goes in the drawing; cause, number and sequence go in words.** If a sentence only describes a shape, delete it and draw the shape. _Why:_ 919071r's "O writer" note; ¶654.
6. **Key parts with short letters on or beside the part, and reuse the same letters in the notes.** No long labels on the drawing. _Why:_ 919057 (am, cb), 919001 (o n m), Madrid I, ¶549.
7. **Number any sequence explicitly** (1, 2, 3 or a, b, c). Never rely on position. _Why:_ 919009r starts at the lower left and needs its 1st–4th labels.
8. **The main figure goes in first, largest, and sets the layout.** Details and notes fill around it in compact blocks next to what they explain, and white space is reserved on purpose. _Why:_ RCT on Manuscript A layout; the density of working sheets is accidental.
9. **Every note says something true and specific** (a measurement, cause, step or instruction) in readable, non-mirrored text. No lorem, no script fonts. _Why:_ his notes are content (912284, 919009). Pseudo-script is the main pastiche tell.
10. **Line, not fill.** Model form with tapered parallel hatching sloping top-left to bottom-right, only where form needs explaining. No gradients, no drop shadows, no cross-hatching everywhere. _Why:_ Getty on the left-handed slope; Kemp on systematic light and shade.
11. **Construction appears only if it really generated the drawing.** Keep it as a lighter under-layer: axes, grids, modules, compass divisions. _Why:_ 919057, 919132 and 912284 are real geometry, and underdrawings are schematic (Technè). Fake geometry is decoration.
12. **Monochrome ink on a light ground, plus at most one accent** (red chalk or a single wash), used only to separate a system or show depth. _Why:_ colour is usually absent (919102); 912281 uses its wash to clarify space.
13. **State scale or unit in every plate:** a dimension with units, or a declared module. _Why:_ 912319 measures in head-units, Codex Leicester f. 9v in braccia, 912284 in miles and bearings.
14. **Never simulate age:** no foxing, burns, halos, stains or faux parchment. _Why:_ browning and haloing are iron-gall corrosion, the ink was black when new, and the paper was clean rag paper (IFLA, Met, RCT).
15. **Work in passes, in this order:** swift rough scribble, clean ink plate, letter key and notes, modern annotation overlay last. _Why:_ ¶579 and ¶596 (whole first, finish selectively); 912281's staged build into a clean template; 919132's fair copy. The overlay marks intent on a drawing that already reads on its own.
