# The anomalies of ink

Each entry is one thing a real pen does that a plotter does not. For each one this file gives:

- **Cause**: the physical act behind it.
- **Engine**: where the engine makes it, and the knob that controls it.
- **Range**: the range that reads as a hand.
- **Seen**: how the critic sees it.
- **Tells**: the plotter tell (missing) and the costume tell (overdone).

Numbers match the specimen sheet in [`../specimens/anomalies/plate.png`](../specimens/anomalies/plate.png). Regenerate it with `node packages/mai/scripts/specimen.ts`.

The rule behind the whole list is that **the act leaves marks, but time is a separate choice.**

- **Anomalies of the act** are always on. They come from the hand, the pen, the ink and the paper as the line is made.
- **Damage of time** (halos, browning, foxing, stains) is off unless a plate sets `time` with a `timeReason`. The research calls faux age the first tell of a fake (`ideas/kiruk-codex/research/01-leonardo.md` §1, §5, R14).

## Damage of time (off by default)

| Anomaly   | Engine                                                                              | Allowed when                                                                                                                                       |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Halo      | `InkSpec.haloStrength` × `time`: a pale ring in `InkSpec.halo` colour               | `PlateSpec.time > 0` and a `timeReason` the founder accepts (`bleed.time` is always a LOOK item). The constructor refuses `time` without a reason. |
| Browning  | Iron-gall moves toward chestnut with `time` (research 01 §1: black → warm chestnut) | Same                                                                                                                                               |

## Reading a fake at 4×

Look at the crops before the whole sheet. In order:

1. **Ends.** Do strokes land on their weight and taper or hook, or end square?
2. **Joins.** Gaps and slight overlaps, or eye-shaped lenses where two tapers meet?
3. **Edges.** Is there a slight wander and fine wisps on the open side, or a vector edge?
4. **Rhythm.** Do strokes darken after a dip and pale as it drains, or is every stroke the same?
5. **Short strokes.** Do they read as ticks, or as leaves, commas or tadpoles?
6. **Blots.** Is a blot an event (after a dip, at a rest), or does every start have one?
7. **Dry media.** Is it grain on a pale tone, or a barcode?
